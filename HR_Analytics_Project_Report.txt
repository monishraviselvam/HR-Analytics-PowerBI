Project Title:
HR Analytics Dashboard using Power BI

Project Objective:

To analyze employee demographics, workforce distribution, hiring trends, and employee terminations using Power BI, and provide meaningful insights to support HR decision-making.

Dataset Description

The HR dataset contains 22,214 employee records with 14 columns, including employee ID, gender, race, department, job title, location, city, state, birth date, hire date and termination date. The dataset is used to analyze workforce demographics, employee distribution, hiring patterns and termination trends.

Data Cleaning
- Checked and corrected data types for all columns.
- Converted date columns into appropriate date formats.
- Removed the trailing UTC text from termination dates.
- Preserved blank termination dates for active employees.
- Verified employee IDs for duplicates.
- Confirmed that all 22,214 employee records have unique IDs.

Key Performance Indicators
1. Total Employees – 22,214
2. HQ Work % – 75.25%
3. Remote Work % – 24.75%
4. Average Age – 38.43 years
5. Active Employees – 18,285
6. Terminated Employees – 3,929
7. Termination Rate – 17.69%

Business Insights:

1.75.25% employees work at Headquarters, while 24.75% employees work remotely. This indicates that most employees follow an office-based work arrangement.

2.The workforce consists mainly of employees aged 25–54, with an average employee age of 38.43 years. Employees aged 55 and above represent the smallest age group.

3.Engineering is the largest department, with approximately 6,700 employees, followed by Accounting with approximately 3,300 employees. This indicates a strong workforce concentration in Engineering.

4.Out of 22,214 employees, 18,285 are currently active and 3,929 have been terminated. The overall termination rate is 17.69%, indicating that approximately 18% of the workforce has left the organization.

5.Auditing has the highest department termination rate at 23%, followed by Legal and R&D at 20%. Marketing has the lowest rate at 15%. These departments may require further investigation to understand employee retention challenges.

6.Cleveland has the highest employee concentration, with approximately 16,900 employees. Other cities such as Chicago, Philadelphia and Pittsburgh have significantly smaller employee counts.

7.The company maintained a relatively stable hiring trend from 2001 to 2019, with annual hires generally ranging between 1,050 and 1,150. Hiring peaked in 2018 with 1,147 employees.

8.Male employees account for 1,960 terminations, while female employees account for 1,866 and non-conforming employees account for 103. The termination counts between male and female employees are relatively close.

9.Research Analyst 2 has the highest employee count among the top 10 job titles, with 754 employees, followed by Business Analyst with 708 employees. This shows a significant workforce presence in analytical roles.

10.Approximately 3,000 terminated employees were from Headquarters, compared to around 900 from Remote locations. This indicates higher termination counts among office-based employees.

11.The average employee age across departments ranges between 38 and 39 years. Support, R&D, Engineering and Auditing have a slightly higher average age of 39 years.

12.Employees hired in 2006 recorded the highest number of terminations, with 221 employees, while the 2000 hiring cohort recorded the lowest, with 31 employees.

Recommendations from insights:

1.The company should conduct employee feedback surveys, identify reasons for employee turnover, and introduce retention strategies, especially in departments with higher termination rates.

2.The company should evaluate employee productivity and satisfaction across both Headquarters and Remote work arrangements. Based on the findings, it can develop flexible work policies to improve employee satisfaction and operational efficiency.

3.The company should focus on strategic workforce planning by evaluating employee strength across departments and identifying future skill requirements. Training and development programs should be introduced to support employee growth and organizational needs.

4.The company should continue data-driven recruitment planning by monitoring annual hiring trends, evaluating workforce requirements, and aligning recruitment activities with departmental needs.

Conclusion

The HR Analytics Dashboard provides an overview of employee demographics, workforce distribution, hiring trends and employee terminations. The analysis highlights workforce concentration, department-wise employee distribution and termination patterns. These insights can help the HR team improve workforce planning, employee retention and recruitment strategies.
